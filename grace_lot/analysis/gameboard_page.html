Gameboard dynamics

How a concept graph runs as a board game on a 12×12 grid. Every trace below comes from the real engine (embodied_lot.ConceptGraph with dynamics = "gameboard"), recorded by grace_lot/analysis/gameboard_traces.py.

The boardThe imagination is a 12×12 grid of cells. Bodies are tokens that sit on whole cells, and everything the graph imagines is drawn onto the board once per tick. A cell nobody draws is unknown (0.5).
Observers lookAny node that is the source of a dashed edge (and not a target) is an observer, and so is its whole subtree: the pattern it looks for in its own region of the board. A parent observer reads the pattern its children form, composed the way its type composes them. Observers read last tick's drawing and are never drawn.
Imagined nodes drawEvery other node is imagined and drawn in its region, weighted by its gate: yang bands towards 1, yin bands towards 0 (known yin). A motion trigram specifies motion only, so it draws nothing static. For observers, yin bands are masks: they score only their yang bands.
Dashed edges take a tickA dashed edge sets its target's gate to what the source sees; the change acts from the next tick. Targets start at 0.5: not yet known. A target can be a leaf or a whole group, and a group's gate scales everything in it: how strongly it is drawn and whether its motion leaves fire.
Templates place contentIn plan(template, content) (and in a body), the template says where its content goes. A static template's yang bands are slots, and a serial template's strips are slots. With probability p (the template's gate) the content goes in the slots; with 1 − p it covers the whole region unchanged.
Dynamic templates turn contentA motion leaf in a template is read as a direction: the content is turned so its "up" faces that way. top/V* turns it upside down, a serial template gives each strip its own facing, and a parallel template adds its directions (up + left faces up-left, in stair-stepped cell bands). Turns nested inside turned content add up. Template motion moves nothing.
Motion leaves move thingsWhen an imagined motion leaf (*) switches on (strictly above 0.5), it moves what is in its space: bodies as whole tokens, and mass, the cells drawn outside bodies, which stay displaced while the tree keeps drawing them there. Each trigram has its own kind: top/bottom move everything together (bulk); upper/lower peel layers off the leading edge one per tick (mass spreads, bodies queue); center drives the two halves apart and periphery drives them together; universal gives every cell a random push each tick (Brownian). While a leaf stays on, whatever walks into its space is moved too. Inside turned content the motion is turned.
Bodies step cell by cellA body has no numbers of its own: the tree defines it. It fills the region its place in the tree gives it (its strip, its parent body's window, or its turned frame). Each order earns gate × motion strength cells of credit per tick (the motion leaf's gate times its groups' gates; upper/lower have half the strength of top/bottom) and spends it one cell at a time, so a weaker gate means slower moves. Orders add up, and bodies stay on the board. A body inside turned content lives and moves in that turned frame.

Labels: top/H* is a trigram with orientation H; * means dynamic (a motion leaf). Motion runs from a trigram's figure side across to the background: top/V* moves down, bottom/V* up, top/H* left and bottom/H* right. Inside a template, a motion leaf instead turns the content to face that way. serial_H splits its region into left and right strips, and a body fills the strip it sits in.
